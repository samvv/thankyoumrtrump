By mid-April 2026, 29 people had died in the custody of Immigration and
Customs Enforcement since the fiscal year began in October, passing the
previous full-year record of 28 set in 2004. Reporting described detention
centers that were overcrowded, hot and short of medical care, and one death was
ruled a homicide by asphyxia. The Department of Homeland Security says there
has been no spike in deaths, pointing to the much larger number of people
detained, and that detainees receive comprehensive medical care. Its inspector
general has opened a review.
